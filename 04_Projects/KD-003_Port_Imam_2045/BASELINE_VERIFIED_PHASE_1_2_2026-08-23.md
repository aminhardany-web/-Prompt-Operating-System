# KD-003 — Port Imam Khomeini 2045
## Verified Baseline — Phase 1 & Phase 2

**Status:** VERIFIED BASELINE / NOT FROZEN AS FINAL
**Verification date:** 2026-08-23
**Scope:** International port strategy, reference ports, official port-management methodologies, university/programme evidence, and implications for KD-003.

### 1. Baseline rule
This document is the controlled baseline for the verified portion of the Phase 1–2 research package. It supersedes unsupported wording in the incoming archive while preserving analytical categories that remain useful for further research. It is not a claim that one universal strategic-port methodology exists.

### 2. Verified institutional foundations

**World Bank — Port Reform Toolkit, Third Edition (2025).** The official World Bank page states that ports are continuously transformed by macroeconomic, technological and environmental changes; the third edition addresses supply-chain resilience, climate change, corporate responsibility and an increasingly digital world. The Toolkit is presented as a comprehensive guide for port reform and improvement and explicitly covers identifying trends, structured change management, balancing efficiency/equity, risk sharing, sustainable development and newer sector challenges.
Source: https://www.worldbank.org/en/topic/transport/publication/port-reform-toolkit

**UNCTAD — TrainForTrade Port Management Programme.** The official programme is 240 hours over two years, uses eight modules, and requires a final dissertation. Course content is adapted to local contexts; the dissertation identifies a real challenge in the participants' port and proposes concrete management solutions.
Source: https://tft.unctad.org/en/programmes/port-management/

**UNCTAD Port Management Series — Volume 14 (2026).** Officially published 26 February 2026; it reports case studies from the English-speaking TrainForTrade network for 2019–2024 and describes the programme's focus on stronger port communities, efficiency, cooperation, competitiveness, institutional capacity and sustainable development.
Source: https://tft.unctad.org/en/2026/02/26/unctad-port-management-series-volume-14/

**OECD — Supply-chain resilience.** The phrase “agile, adaptable and aligned supply chains” is an OECD formulation for supply-chain resilience. For KD-003 it is valid as a supply-chain resilience concept, not as a single formal port-strategy methodology.

**IAPH — Port Readiness Level for Marine Fuels (PRL-MF).** IAPH materials define a structured readiness scale for specific marine fuels, progressing from fuel relevance assessment and stakeholder interest through policy/roadmap, development, demonstration and deployment. This is a specific readiness/assessment tool, not a universal port-strategy framework.

### 3. Verified reference-port evidence

**Port of Rotterdam.** Official Digital Report 2025 evidence documents data sharing, standardized APIs for ETA/ATA exchange, ship-to-shore data flows, corridor collaboration with other ports, AI-supported decision making and community-driven operational testing. This supports the classification “data-enabled/digital port transformation with corridor collaboration.” The phrase “data-driven port orchestration” is an analytical label, not an official named methodology.
Source: https://publications.portofrotterdam.com/digital-report-2025/reflections

**Port of Antwerp-Bruges.** The official Smart Port page defines a smart port as bringing together economy, climate and people and documents digital twin, sensors, autonomous drones, smart cameras, NxtPort and public/private ecosystem collaboration.
Source: https://www.portofantwerpbruges.com/en/our-port/port-future/smart-port

**Port of Hamburg / ChainPORT.** Official HPA material documents the SmartPORT concept and the creation of ChainPORT as an international network for port information exchange, benchmarking and joint development of innovative solutions. The existence of ChainPORT and its stated objectives are verified; the original source is older and should not be treated as a new 2026 initiative.
Source: https://www.hafen-hamburg.de/en/press1/news/foundation-of-the-global-port-network-chainport--34590/

**Port of Los Angeles.** The Clean Air Action Plan and the port's zero-emission transition are official, verifiable strategic initiatives. Broader wording such as “regulatory-driven strategy” is analytical unless supported by an explicit strategy document.

**Singapore MPA.** The official Maritime Digitalisation Playbook contains a Digital Transformation Framework organised around why, what and how, including planning, core-process digitalisation, new digital offerings and management foundations covering structure/processes, people, data/systems and partner ecosystem.
Source: https://www.mpa.gov.sg/api/media/76a32d69-4414-40ea-8d50-aefd5bb0a106

### 4. University/programme evidence

**Erasmus University Rotterdam / Maritime Economics and Logistics (MEL).** Official Erasmus material establishes MEL as a multidisciplinary maritime programme connecting port/shipping economics, logistics, supply-chain management and related operational and business topics. Older official programme material explicitly references operational excellence in harbours and networks, hinterland/security, shipping/finance, information infrastructure and environment. Current programme details must still be rechecked when a specific 2026 course reading list is claimed.

**NTU Singapore — MSc Maritime Studies.** Official programme evidence supports maritime economics, shipping management, port management, maritime logistics, environmental shipping and sustainability/digitalisation as curriculum domains. Specific required books must be established from course-level reading lists before being called prescribed texts.

**World Maritime University (WMU).** WMU remains a high-priority reference institution for maritime affairs, policy, governance and management. A claim that a specific WMU course in 2026 uses a specific book or methodology requires direct course-level evidence and is not frozen by this baseline alone.

### 7. Verified analytical baseline for KD-003

The current evidence supports investigation of an integrated strategic stack comprising:

**A. Governance and reform** — port governance, change management, regulation, stakeholder alignment.

**B. Port economics and competitiveness** — market position, network role, pricing/cost drivers, investment and competition.

**C. Maritime economics and shipping markets** — shipping demand/supply, trade, fleet/route dynamics and market risk.

**D. Port logistics and hinterland integration** — terminal performance, road/rail interfaces, supply-chain visibility and network connectivity.

**E. Digital transformation** — data governance, process digitalisation, PCS/TOS integration, interoperability, AI and cybersecurity.

**F. Resilience** — scenario-based disruption analysis, recovery capability, redundancy, supply-chain continuity and adaptive planning.

**G. Energy transition and sustainability** — emissions reduction, low-emission fuel readiness, energy systems, climate adaptation and environmental/social performance.

**H. Human capital** — local training, managerial capability, technical skills, change leadership and institutional learning.

**I. Strategic portfolio and implementation governance** — prioritisation, investment sequencing, KPIs, benefits tracking and continuous feedback.

**Classification:** INFERENCE based on the verified institutional and port evidence above. It is not an official single framework published by one institution.

### 8. Implication for Port Imam Khomeini

For KD-003, SWOT/PESTEL/BSC should not be discarded. They should be treated as supporting diagnostic tools inside a broader evidence-based strategy process rather than as the whole methodology. The primary strategic architecture should be built from verified institutional components, then adapted to Port Imam Khomeini's governance, cargo structure, hinterland, rail/road connectivity, investment constraints, regulatory environment, digital maturity, energy profile and resilience needs.
