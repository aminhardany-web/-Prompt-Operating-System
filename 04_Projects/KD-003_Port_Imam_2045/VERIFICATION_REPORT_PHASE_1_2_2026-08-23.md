# KD-003 — Verification Report, Phase 1–2

**Date:** 2026-08-23
**Status:** PARTIALLY VERIFIED / CANONICAL FACTS UPDATED / PORT-CASE CLAIMS REMAIN EVIDENCE-SCOPED

## Executive finding

The incoming Phase 1–2 research package contains a strong and useful direction, but several formulations in the submitted text were too categorical. The official-source review confirms a substantial core of the proposed evidence base while downgrading unsupported universal claims.

## VERIFIED — official primary sources

### 1. World Bank Port Reform Toolkit, 2025 Third Edition

Verified. The World Bank's official publication page states publication in 2025 and describes the Third Edition as an enhanced toolbox for port reform and improvement. It explicitly identifies supply-chain resilience, climate change, corporate responsibility and digitalisation as contemporary drivers, and gives modules including Trends, Change Management and Port Governance. The change-management module uses a structured five-step cycle: assessment, planning, implementation, performance measurement, and continuous feedback.

**Classification:** FACT
**Use in KD-003:** Primary policy/implementation reference for port reform, governance, change management, risk sharing, sustainability and contemporary port-sector trends.

### 2. UNCTAD TrainForTrade Port Management Programme

Verified. The official programme page states 240 hours over two years, eight modules, a final dissertation, and local-context adaptation. The eight modules cover international trade and transport; organisation of a port system; functioning of a port system; future challenges; methods and tools of port management; economic/commercial management; administrative/legal management; and technical management/human-resources development.

**Classification:** FACT
**Use in KD-003:** Reference for management capacity building, local adaptation, benchmarking/problem solving, and executive training.

### 3. UNCTAD Port Management Series Volume 14 (2026)

Verified. UNCTAD published Volume 14 in February 2026. It presents case studies from the English-speaking TrainForTrade network for 2019–2024 and explicitly discusses efficiency, competitiveness, cooperation, institutional capacity, knowledge exchange and practical reform plans.

**Classification:** FACT
**Use in KD-003:** Current evidence for applied port-management capacity building and cross-port learning.

### 4. OECD Supply Chain Resilience Review 2025

Verified as an OECD publication. The source contains the section “Beyond resilience: Agile, adaptable and aligned supply chains.”

**Classification:** FACT
**Use in KD-003:** Candidate conceptual reference for supply-chain resilience. The exact transferability to a port strategy should be treated as analysis, not as an OECD port-specific framework.

### 5. IAPH Port Readiness Level for Marine Fuels (PRL-MF)

Verified. IAPH describes PRL-MF as a self-assessment and communication tool for a port community to determine current ability to bunker vessels with individual low-emission fuels and to identify steps needed to reach intended future capability.

**Classification:** FACT
**Use in KD-003:** Candidate operational readiness assessment for alternative/low-emission marine fuels.

### 6. Port of Rotterdam strategic direction

Verified from the Port of Rotterdam Authority. The published strategy identifies four focal points: balance with society; climate neutrality/circularity; resilience, security of supply and strategic autonomy; and future-proof earning power. The Authority states that its strategy is translated into 16 specific targets and monitored through key results and initiatives. Its 2025 digital report additionally states that AI supports decision-making, data sharing underpins a connected ecosystem, cybersecurity is a priority, and renewable energy investment is ongoing.

**Classification:** FACT
**Analytical implication:** Rotterdam is a valid evidence case for a multi-pillar strategy combining sustainability, resilience, digitalisation, stakeholder collaboration and economic performance.

### 7. Singapore Maritime Digitalisation Playbook

Verified from Singapore MPA. The Playbook includes a Digital Transformation Framework organised around the why, what and how of digital transformation, plus self-assessment and proposal templates. The MPA page also links current digitalisation programmes and digital-corridor initiatives.

**Classification:** FACT
**Analytical implication:** Singapore is a valid evidence case for structured digital-transformation planning, maturity assessment, business alignment, cybersecurity, data/systems and partner-ecosystem considerations.

### 8. Port of Antwerp-Bruges smart-port model

Verified. The official port describes a smart port as bringing together economy, climate and people, with smart technologies and digitalisation playing a key role. It documents digital twin work, sensors, autonomous drones, smart cameras, 5G, NxtPort and ecosystem/innovation-platform cooperation.

**Classification:** FACT
**Analytical implication:** Evidence supports describing Antwerp-Bruges as an ecosystem-oriented smart-port case with integrated digital and sustainability dimensions.

### 9. Port of Hamburg — SmartPORT and ChainPORT

Verified for the existence and purpose of ChainPORT and its relationship to the SmartPORT strategy. HPA describes ChainPORT as a network for information exchange, joint innovation and benchmarking among leading ports.

**Classification:** FACT
**Analytical implication:** Valid case for inter-port learning/networking; it should not be inflated into a universal strategic framework used by all leading ports.

### 10. Port of Los Angeles — Clean Air Action Plan

Verified. The official Port of Los Angeles page states that the San Pedro Bay Ports Clean Air Action Plan is a collaborative strategy with Port of Long Beach, adopted in 2006 and updated in 2010 and 2017, and that the 2017 update accelerates progress toward a zero-emission future while protecting port competitiveness.

**Classification:** FACT
**Analytical implication:** Valid case for regulatory/public-policy-driven environmental transition. It does not, by itself, establish the broader claim of a complete 2026 strategic model.

### 11. Erasmus University Rotterdam — MSc Maritime Economics and Logistics (MEL)

Verified from the official MEL programme and course-description pages. The programme integrates Economics, Data Science, Management Science, Sustainability, Port Management and Economics, Shipping Economics and Policy, Logistics and Supply Chain Management, Shipping and Transport Finance, Maritime Law, Maritime Logistics and a thesis/project component. The Port Management and Economics course explicitly targets port authority management and evaluation of port policies; Shipping Economics and Policy includes market analysis, pricing, policy analysis, regression and time-series techniques.

**Classification:** FACT
**Use in KD-003:** Strong academic reference for an integrated port/shipping/logistics management curriculum.

### 12. NTU Singapore — MSc Maritime Studies

Verified from the official NTU page. Current curriculum includes Maritime Economics, Shipping Management, Ship Operations/Brokering/Chartering, Marine Insurance and Claims, Maritime Logistics, Port Management, Environmental Shipping, Sustainable and Green Finance, and Sustainability and Digitalisation. The university explicitly notes that course offerings can change each academic year.

**Classification:** FACT
**Use in KD-003:** Strong academic reference for the integration of port management, maritime economics, logistics, sustainability, digitalisation and finance.

### 13. Maritime Economics — Martin Stopford, 3rd edition

Verified through Routledge's 2026 catalogue. The catalogue identifies the third edition and its core coverage of shipping markets, shipping cycles, finance and maritime trade.

**Classification:** FACT
**Use in KD-003:** Foundational shipping-economics reference; it should not be presented as a current digital-port or smart-port manual.

### 14. Port Economics, Management and Policy — 2nd edition

Verified in Routledge's 2026 catalogue as a second edition title by Theo Notteboom, Athanasios Pallis and Jean-Paul Rodrigue. Exact publication status and bibliographic details must use the current publisher record at time of citation.

**Classification:** FACT (bibliographic); analytical ranking as “most important” is NOT verified as a universal fact.

## CLAIMS DOWNGRADED / NOT VERIFIED AS UNIVERSAL FACT

1. “Leading ports in 2026 no longer use SWOT/BSC.”
   - **Status:** NOT VERIFIED.
   - Reason: official sources reviewed do not support a universal exclusion claim.
   - Correct use: SWOT/BSC may be treated as legacy/adjunct tools that should not be relied on alone when the strategy requires dynamic, ecosystem, resilience, digital and transition analysis.

2. “All leading ports use the same exact methods.”
   - **Status:** NOT VERIFIED.
   - Correct use: multiple common themes appear across cases, but each port uses its own strategy and institutional model.

3. “Port Reform Toolkit is itself a universal Strategic Port Planning Framework.”
   - **Status:** TOO BROAD.
   - Correct use: it is a World Bank toolkit for port reform and improvement, including trends, change management, governance and related modules; KD-003 may use its components as a strategic design input.

4. “The five cited books/frameworks are formally endorsed by all named universities and international bodies.”
   - **Status:** NOT VERIFIED.
   - Correct use: distinguish official institutional publications from widely used academic reference works and from recommended/required course texts.

5. “A single combination covers 70–80% of port knowledge.”
   - **Status:** UNSUPPORTED.

6. “The ten selected ports are the ten best ports in the world.”
   - **Status:** UNSUPPORTED unless a specific ranking criterion and source are stated.
   - Correct use: “ten reference cases selected for strategic-method comparison.”

## Current synthesis — verified vs analytical

### Verified cross-cutting pattern
The evidence supports a recurring bundle of themes across the reviewed institutional and port sources:
- sustainability/decarbonisation;
- resilience and security of supply;
- digitalisation/data/AI;
- stakeholder and ecosystem collaboration;
- infrastructure and operational performance;
- institutional and human-capital capacity.

### Analytical synthesis for KD-003
A defensible Port Imam Khomeini strategy should therefore be designed as a multi-layer evidence system rather than a single textbook method:

1. Port reform and governance assessment.
2. Port economics, competitiveness and network position.
3. Maritime/shipping market analysis.
4. Supply-chain and hinterland integration.
5. Digital maturity, data governance and process digitalisation.
6. Resilience and disruption scenarios.
7. Energy transition/decarbonisation readiness.
8. Human-capital and organisational capability.
9. Strategic portfolio, investment and implementation governance.

This is an **INFERENCE**, not a quoted universal methodology.

## Freeze decision

**Phase 1–2 source verification: PARTIALLY PASSED.**

**Canonical facts:** may be promoted into the reference registry with source URLs.
**Analytical synthesis:** may enter the KD-003 research layer, clearly labelled `INFERENCE`.
**Unsupported universal claims:** rejected or downgraded.
**Port-by-port methodology labels:** remain evidence-scoped and should be validated case-by-case before being used as formal benchmark methodology.
